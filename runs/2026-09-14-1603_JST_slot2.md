Even if one assumes this, though, it’s important to see that leaders of frontier AI companies have, according to their model of the world, obvious self-interested reasons to care about AI safety. 

This is not just because AI takeover would obviously be bad for everyone, but because there is a vast space of possible but less dramatic loss-of-control incidents that would be catastrophic for them and their companies, not just financially but also reputationally and legally. 

Suppose AI systems built by Anthropic literally take over the internet and cause hundreds of billions of dollars in damage, a scenario Amodei raises here. This would be an absolutely terrible outcome for him personally in innumerable ways. 

So, AI company leaders have clear self-interested reasons to push for safety regulations that they can’t achieve unilaterally. To recognise this doesn’t mean abandoning the appropriate cynicism one should direct at tech CEOs (or any other powerful people). It just means appreciating the basic structure of collective action problems.
